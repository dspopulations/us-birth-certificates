# Evidence needed for study claims

**Original date:** 7 July 2026. Historical research note, revised for clarity in October 2026.

This review separated descriptive findings from estimates that depend on priors, source transport or model restrictions. The original rankings and proposed paper outlines are superseded by the [project plan](../plans/readme.md).

Recorded counts and their denominators can support statements about what certificates report. They cannot establish the number of true missed cases. A classifier-selected cohort can show how a ranking and quota behave; it cannot validate its own assumed prevalence.

The selection models separate counterfactual prevalence, prenatal reduction and recording through priors. The core models use fewer stages. Surveillance-anchored models add another evidence source but also assumptions about its uncertainty, overlapping windows and national coverage.

Subgroup contrasts need particular care. Differences in recorded prevalence can reflect age mix, true live-birth prevalence, recording or false positives. An age-standardised contrast still combines those mechanisms. A low posterior correlation or a small interval does not establish that one mechanism has been measured separately.

For a write-up, state the target quantity before reporting a number. Give the case definition, birth denominator, period, missing-value policy and source of each restriction. Report changes under defensible alternative assumptions alongside posterior intervals. Explain which uncertainty those intervals omit.
