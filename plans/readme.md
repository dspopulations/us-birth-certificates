# Plans

> [!WARNING]
> This is work in progress. All data and models are preliminary.

This study aims to provide updated estimates of the numbers of babies born with Down syndrome in the U.S. and to explore factors influencing births and how they may be changing over time. To mitigate the problem of possible biases associated with systemic underreporting in birth certificate data, we are using machine learning techniques to identify likely true cases of Down syndrome in birth certificate data that were not recorded as having the condition (missed cases).

More specifically, we aim to:

1.	Document the numbers and characteristics of babies with Down syndrome identified in birth certificate data from 1989 to 2024 and compare this data to surveillance-based estimates.
2.	Identify predictors of recorded cases of Down syndrome in birth certificate data from 2016 to 2024 by training a predictive model, selecting the most important predictors and evaluating the model’s performance.
3.	Use the predictive model to identify the most likely missing cases in in birth certificate data from 2016 to 2024 and estimate all births of Down syndrome (recorded + missed).
4.	Document the numbers and characteristics of babies with Down syndrome identified or predicted to by most likely missed cases in birth certificate data from 2016 to 2024, compare this data to surveillance-based estimates, and compare population-level estimates of co-occurring conditions with previous estimates.
5.	Develop a statistical model to explore and estimate factors influencing live births of babies with Down syndrome and how they may be evolving over time.
